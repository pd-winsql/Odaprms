# Technopreneurship 101: Week 6 and Week 7 Class Prep

## The whole lesson in one idea

A business succeeds against competitors by giving customers a clear reason to choose it. That reason may be a lower cost, a product customers see as better or different, a better experience, or a strong position in a carefully chosen market. To build this advantage, the business must understand its competitors, use the marketing mix, know its market structure, and focus its limited resources on a suitable first market.

## 1. Competitive advantage

### Definition

Competitive advantage is an attribute or capability that allows a business to outperform its rivals. It helps the company attract customers, create value, and earn better profit margins.

For an advantage to last, competitors must find it difficult to copy, replace, or counter.

### Common sources

- Technology or production methods that competitors cannot access
- Intellectual property, such as patents
- Highly skilled employees and organizational knowledge
- Scarce natural resources
- A trusted brand and strong reputation
- The ability to produce at a lower cost
- Unique product features
- Excellent customer knowledge and service
- Strategic partnerships

### Recitation-ready answer

> Competitive advantage is the reason customers choose one business over its competitors. It can come from lower cost, superior or unique value, technology, employees, brand reputation, customer service, or other resources that competitors cannot easily copy.

## 2. Comparative advantage and differential advantage

### Comparative advantage

A company has a comparative advantage when it can produce or offer a comparable product more efficiently or at a lower cost than competitors. The product does not have to be superior. Its advantage is efficiency, cost, or price.

**Question to ask:** Can the business offer similar value at a lower cost?

**Example:** Two gasoline products have the same octane rating, but one is cheaper. A price-sensitive customer selects the cheaper one.

### Differential advantage

A company has a differential advantage when customers perceive its product or service as superior or meaningfully different. The difference may come from technology, patented features, quality, convenience, employees, service, or brand identity.

**Question to ask:** Does the customer choose it because it feels better, more unique, more convenient, or more trusted?

**Example:** A customer buys a more expensive gasoline brand because they believe their car runs better with it.

### Fast comparison

| Comparative advantage | Differential advantage |
|---|---|
| Focuses on efficiency and lower cost | Focuses on uniqueness or perceived superiority |
| Can offer a comparable product for less | Offers a reason other than price to prefer the product |
| Often supports cost leadership | Often supports differentiation |
| Example: lowest-priced 93-octane fuel | Example: fuel perceived to improve engine performance |

### Answers to the Week 6 situations

1. **Sander chooses Petron XCS because his car seems to run better:** Differential advantage. He chooses it for perceived performance, not the lowest price.
2. **Nimfa chooses Caltex Platinum because it has the lowest price:** Comparative advantage. Comparable fuel is available for less.
3. **Zandro chooses Caltex because it is near his favorite restaurant:** Differential advantage. Convenient location creates distinct value for him.
4. **Sander compares the Apple ecosystem with Samsung's S Pen and camera features:** Differential advantage. The decision concerns unique features and experience.
5. **Sander chooses Vivo for flagship features at a more affordable price:** Mainly comparative advantage. It gives him comparable high-end value for less money. You can add that the feature-price combination also supports Vivo's differentiation, but the scenario emphasizes affordability.

## 3. Building competitive advantage

The goal is to create a difference that matters to customers and that competitors cannot easily match.

The decks point to these methods:

- **Capture knowledge:** Collect experts' practices and advice through knowledge tools or secure forums. Organizational knowledge improves decisions and processes.
- **Become a low-cost producer:** Reduce production costs so the business can charge an affordable price while maintaining acceptable quality.
- **Develop valuable features:** Focus product development on benefits customers truly value. Unique, hard-to-copy features reduce the threat of imitation or substitutes.
- **Use strategic partnerships:** Partners can provide expertise, components, technology, distribution, or other resources that support innovation.
- **Know and serve customers well:** Research customers, improve their experience and service, gather customer information through sales channels, and personalize marketing.

The deck also identifies four broad areas in which a business may lead: **cost, quality, innovation, and customer experience**.

### Likely question: What makes an advantage sustainable?

> A competitive advantage is sustainable when competitors cannot easily duplicate, substitute, or neutralize it. Patents, specialized knowledge, strong customer relationships, rare resources, and a trusted brand can make an advantage more difficult to copy.

## 4. Why a business must know its competitors

Competitor knowledge is essential to a successful marketing strategy. It helps a business:

- Identify competitors' strengths and weaknesses
- Find unmet customer needs and gaps in the market
- Anticipate lower prices, new features, promotions, and market entry
- Decide how to improve its own offer
- Avoid being surprised by a competitor with a stronger value proposition

### Answers to Sander's activity

**Is there no need for Sander to know his competitors? Why?**

> Sander still needs to know them, even if none operates nearby yet. Potential competitors can enter later, and customers may already have alternatives or substitutes. Studying them early helps him protect his market, improve his offer, and prepare a response.

**What should Sander do once competitors are operating?**

> He should compare their products, prices, locations, promotions, customer experience, strengths, and weaknesses. He should listen to customers, protect what already makes his business valuable, and improve areas where competitors offer better value. He should avoid copying blindly and instead create a clear advantage that fits his target market.

## 5. The marketing mix: the four Ps

Competition pushes businesses to improve the four Ps:

- **Product:** What the business offers, including features, quality, design, brand, and benefits
- **Price:** What customers pay and the pricing strategy used
- **Place:** Where and how the product reaches customers, including location and distribution
- **Promotion:** How the business communicates and persuades through advertising, selling, content, and promotions

### Simple example

A coffee shop may offer locally sourced drinks (**product**), student-friendly prices (**price**), a location beside a campus plus delivery (**place**), and social-media discounts (**promotion**).

## 6. Types of competitors

### Direct competitor

A direct competitor sells the same type of product or service to the same target customers and satisfies the same need in a similar way.

**Deck example:** McDonald's and Burger King.

### Indirect competitor

An indirect competitor offers a different product within a related category, but it can satisfy the same general need.

**Deck example:** McDonald's and Mang Inasal. Both sell prepared meals, although their main products differ.

### Replacement competitor

A replacement competitor, sometimes called a phantom competitor, offers something from a different category on which the customer may spend the same limited money instead. It competes for the customer's budget or occasion.

**Deck example:** Frozen goods from Pampanga's Best can replace a McDonald's purchase when a customer chooses food to prepare at home.

### The test to remember

- **Same solution:** direct competitor
- **Different solution to the same broad need:** indirect competitor
- **Different category competing for the same budget or occasion:** replacement competitor

## 7. Product differentiation and product positioning

### Product differentiation

Product differentiation is the strategy of making a product or service distinct from competing offers. It concerns the actual or perceived features, quality, benefits, design, service, or uniqueness of the offer.

**Main question:** What makes our product different?

**Objective:** Give customers a reason to choose the product over competitors.

### Product positioning

Product positioning is the place a product occupies in the customer's mind relative to competitors. It is the image, feeling, attitude, or impression that the company wants customers to associate with the product.

**Main question:** How do we want the target customer to think about our product?

**Objective:** Establish a clear and desirable perception in the target market.

Positioning follows earlier marketing decisions, including segmentation, target marketing, and differentiation.

### Fast comparison

| Product differentiation | Product positioning |
|---|---|
| Focuses on the offer | Focuses on customer perception |
| Identifies features, benefits, quality, or uniqueness | Builds a particular image in the customer's mind |
| Gives customers a reason to choose | Defines how customers should view the product relative to alternatives |
| Example: a phone has an S Pen | Example: customers view the phone as the best productivity device |

### How they work together

Differentiation provides the evidence for positioning. A company first creates or identifies a meaningful difference, then communicates that difference so the product occupies a strong position in customers' minds. Differentiation is effective when the target market notices, understands, and values it.

## 8. Market structure

Market structure describes how an industry is organized and how firms compete and interact with customers.

### Defining characteristics

- Number and size of sellers
- Type of product and degree of differentiation
- Ease or difficulty of entering and leaving the market
- Distribution of market share
- Number and bargaining power of buyers
- Relationships and interdependence among businesses
- Degree of control over price
- Availability of market information

### Four market structures

| Structure | Sellers | Product | Entry barriers | Price power | Deck examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perfect competition** | Many firms | Homogeneous or identical | Very low | Firms are price takers | Rice and corn agriculture |
| **Monopolistic competition** | Many firms | Similar but differentiated | Relatively low | Limited price-setting power | Restaurants and hotels |
| **Oligopoly** | A few large firms | Standardized or differentiated | Significant | Firms are interdependent | Steel, oil, automobiles |
| **Monopoly** | One firm | Unique product with no close substitute | Entry is blocked or extremely difficult | Firm is a price maker, subject to demand and often regulation | Local utilities |

### Key terms

**Price taker:** A seller has so little individual market power that it generally accepts the market price.

**Price maker:** A seller has substantial influence over price. This does not mean it can charge any price without consequences, because demand, regulation, and substitutes may still limit it.

**Interdependent:** In an oligopoly, one large firm's decision can affect the others, so each firm considers likely competitor reactions.

### Identification shortcuts

- Many sellers plus identical goods: perfect competition
- Many sellers plus branded differences: monopolistic competition
- A few powerful firms that watch each other: oligopoly
- One seller protected by major barriers: monopoly

## 9. Beachhead strategy and beachhead market

### Beachhead strategy

The term comes from a military tactic: secure a small area, strengthen it, and use it as a base for expansion.

In business, a company concentrates limited resources on a small product category or market segment. It aims to win that focused market first, build a strong position, and then expand into larger or related markets.

### Beachhead market

A beachhead market is the small, focused market selected for the first launch. It should fit the product, the company's resources, and the needs of its target customers. Winning it should help the company gain customers, experience, reputation, revenue, or other capabilities needed for later expansion.

### Example

A startup making a campus payment app could first serve one university rather than every school in the country. By solving that university's needs, gaining active users, and proving reliability, it creates a strong base before entering other campuses.

### Why use it?

- Limited resources are not spread too thinly
- The company can understand a specific customer group deeply
- Early success provides proof and learning
- A strong niche can support expansion

### Important caution

A beachhead market must be narrow enough to win but valuable enough to support the next stage. A segment that is too broad wastes resources, while one that is too small or disconnected may offer no path to growth.

## 10. Connections among all the topics

1. **Study competitors** to understand what alternatives customers have.
2. **Examine the four Ps** to compare how firms create and deliver value.
3. **Choose an advantage:** lower cost or meaningful difference.
4. **Differentiate the offer** through features, quality, service, convenience, or another valued benefit.
5. **Position it clearly** in the target customer's mind.
6. **Account for the market structure,** because the number of firms, barriers, and price power affect strategy.
7. **Choose a beachhead market** where the business can focus its resources and establish a strong position.

## 11. Likely oral questions with model answers

### What is the difference between comparative and differential advantage?

> Comparative advantage concerns offering comparable value more efficiently or at a lower cost. Differential advantage concerns offering something customers perceive as unique or superior, such as better features, quality, convenience, service, or brand.

### Can a company have both advantages?

> Yes. A company may produce efficiently while also offering distinctive value. For example, a phone brand may provide high-end features at a lower price. The dominant advantage depends on why customers choose it and how the company competes.

### Why is low price alone sometimes unsustainable?

> Competitors may match the price, and repeated price cuts can reduce profit and quality. A cost advantage lasts only if the company has an underlying efficiency that competitors cannot easily copy.

### Why are differentiation and positioning closely related?

> Differentiation creates a meaningful distinction in the offer. Positioning communicates that distinction and establishes its place in the customer's mind. Without a valued difference, positioning lacks evidence. Without clear positioning, customers may not notice the difference.

### What is the difference between a competitor and a substitute?

> A direct competitor offers nearly the same solution. A substitute satisfies the same need in a different way. Both matter because either can take the customer's money and reduce demand for the business.

### How does market structure affect a business?

> It affects how much control firms have over price, how easy it is for new competitors to enter, how strongly products must be differentiated, and how much each firm must react to competitors.

### Why is an oligopoly interdependent?

> Only a few large firms control much of the market. A change in one firm's price, output, or product can materially affect the others, so each firm anticipates competitor reactions.

### How is a beachhead strategy useful to a startup?

> A startup has limited resources. Focusing on one suitable segment helps it understand customers, build a reputation, prove its product, and develop a strong base before expanding.

## 13. Thirty-second final review

Remember this chain:

> Know the competitors. Choose whether to win through lower cost or meaningful difference. Use the four Ps to deliver that value. Differentiate the product and position it in the customer's mind.
